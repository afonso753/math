\textbf{Prove that every} $\mathbf{n \geq 12}$ \textbf{is the sum of two composite numbers.}

\vspace{0.5cm}

Let $n$ be even such that $n \geq 8$. Then $n-4$ is an even number greatest or equal than $4$, thus a composite number. Therefore, for every even $n \geq 8$ we can write it as the sum of two composite numbers as follows, 
$$n=4+(n-4).$$

Now let $n$ be odd such that $n \geq 13$. Then $n-9$ is an even number greatest or equal to $4$, thus a composite number. Therefore, for every odd $n \geq 13$ we can write it as the sum of two composite numbers as follows,
$$n=9+(n-9).$$

That is, every $n\geq 12$ can be written as the sum of two composite numbers.
